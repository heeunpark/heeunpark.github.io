About Me

I study why organizations facing the same emerging technology pursue different strategic paths. My research examines how firms interpret emerging technologies, how these interpretations shape external capability search, and how they translate into strategic action. I explore these questions through longitudinal research on corporate technological interpretations and strategic responses.

Beyond academia, I founded and operate a Korean barbecue restaurant where introducing a robotic grilling system required redesigning workflows and staff responsibilities. This experience has deepened my interest in how organizations translate technological possibilities into operational realities.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market and am seeking faculty and postdoctoral research opportunities, with availability beginning in 2027.

Job Market Paper

Extension Without Displacement: Interpretive Accretion in the Organizational Meaning of Emerging Technologies
Working draft, November 2026

Emerging technologies can acquire new organizational meanings without displacing what previously made them consequential. Interpretive accretion captures this form of development: new concerns enter while the preceding meaning configuration remains intact. The analysis uses 7,419 substantive AI-related claims from annual Form 10-K filings of large U.S. firms between 2016 and 2025 to construct annual configurations of eight organizational concern dimensions and examine 600 adjacent firm-year transitions. Dominant concerns changed in only 15.2% of transitions, yet interpretive accretion accounted for 29.7% of all transitions and 62.0% of those involving new concern entry. Accretion also recurred across consecutive transitions, such that meanings introduced as novelty in one period could become part of the continuity carried into the next. Novelty therefore need not redirect technological meaning; it can extend an intact prior configuration and, when retained, become part of subsequent continuity. This temporal relation distinguishes accretion from mere coexistence and from reconfiguration, while showing why a present configuration cannot fully reveal the developmental history through which it emerged. For innovation and technology management, this means that the management problem surrounding an emerging technology can broaden without redirecting its established technical or economic rationale.

Keywords: Emerging technologies; Technological frames; Organizational meaning; Interpretive accretion; Artificial intelligence; Technology management
